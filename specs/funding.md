# Funding

## Purpose

IME takes nothing from sales and is not run for profit, but it still costs money to keep online: storing and serving audio, artwork, merch photos, and page designs, plus the servers, database, and services the platform runs on. This domain covers how those costs are shared, at cost, by the people who sell on IME, how they pay, and how the platform shows where the money goes.

The short version for sellers: **you pay what it costs to host what you use, plus an even share of the costs that keep the lights on, and nothing more.** None of that goes to the people who build IME. Anyone who wants to support them can, separately and entirely optionally.

## Roles

- **Sellers** pay their share of hosting costs. A seller is a user who pays for one or more active artists or designs (see below).
- **Listeners** never pay to use IME. They pay only for what they choose to buy.
- **Anyone** can give extra, to the platform or to its developers, and see where the platform's money goes on the public costs page.

## Principles

- **At cost, never at profit.** Contributions and platform donations cover the platform's real bills. None of that money goes to the people who build and run IME, as salary, profit, or margin.
- **Supporting the developers is separate and optional.** Money given to the developers is only ever given to them on purpose, by someone who chose it, and never comes out of hosting contributions or platform donations.
- **Giving more never buys anything.** Extra giving unlocks no features, placement, or perks.
- **Pay for what you use.** Most of a seller's share comes from their own footprint: the files they store and the requests made to serve them.
- **Shared costs are shared evenly.** Costs that belong to no one seller are split evenly across sellers.
- **Never a cut of sales.** A contribution is a share of hosting costs. It is never a percentage of what an artist or designer sells, and nothing is ever deducted from a sale.
- **Open books.** The platform publishes what it costs to run each month and how that cost was split.

## Who pays

- An **artist** is active while it has anything published for sale, or files stored for something that is for sale.
- A **designer's** designs are active while any of them are published, publicly or privately.
- A user who pays any share of an active artist or of their own active designs is a **seller**. A user who manages several artists is still one seller.

### Splitting an artist's costs between managers

- By default, the user who created an artist pays all of that artist's usage.
- The managers of an artist can split its usage between them in any proportion, give it all to one manager, or share it among a few.
- A manager takes on a share only after accepting it, and must have a card on file to do so.
- Every manager of an artist sees how its costs are split.

## How a seller's share is calculated

Each month, a seller's share is the sum of:

1. **Usage**: the cost of storing and serving the files of the artists and designs they pay for (audio masters and stream copies, artwork, release assets, merch photos, design bundles, images used in release designs), at the provider's actual rates, multiplied by their split for each artist.
2. **Shared costs**: the month's platform-wide costs that belong to no one seller, divided evenly by the number of sellers. These include servers, database, email, domain, monitoring, and the ownership guarantee (see below).
3. **Card fees** on that seller's own top-ups.

Serving a seller's files counts as that seller's usage, whoever is listening or downloading. Provider price changes pass through as they are; IME adds no margin. Shared costs are always split evenly between sellers, with no cap. At launch, IME funds the reserve itself so that it pays sellers' shares (see The reserve) until there are enough sellers for shares to be cheap.

## Paying

- Contributions are paid from a **prepaid balance**.
- Before an artist can put anything on sale, or a designer can publish a design, each seller paying for it adds a card and makes a first top-up, unless the reserve is paying sellers' shares (see The reserve). Then nobody needs a card or a top-up to sell.
- At the end of each monthly period, that month's share is deducted from the balance.
- A seller is emailed when their balance falls below an alert threshold they choose, as an amount or a percentage of their last top-up.
- A seller can turn on **auto top-up**: a chosen amount charged to their card each month, or whenever the balance drops below their threshold.
- A seller can see their balance, the running estimate for the current month, and past monthly statements broken into usage, shared costs, and card fees.
- When a seller has nothing active left, they can request a refund of their unused balance.

## When a balance runs out

- If a monthly deduction leaves a balance below zero and auto top-up fails or is off, a **14 day grace period** begins. The seller is emailed at the start, with 7 days left, and with 1 day left.
- If the balance is still below zero when the grace period ends, the artists and designs that seller pays for come off sale, and their uploads pause. Every manager of an affected artist is emailed.
- Paying what is owed and topping up puts everything back on sale.
- If nothing is paid within **90 days** of coming off sale, files nobody owns are deleted. Files that listeners own are kept.

### The ownership guarantee

Listeners keep what they own even after a seller stops paying or leaves IME. The files they own keep being stored and served, and that cost moves into shared costs as its own line on the public costs page. It is small: lossless audio costs around 1.5 cents per GB per month to store, with no bandwidth charges, so a 20 album discography costs roughly 20 to 25 cents a month. Designs that releases still use are kept the same way.

## Giving more

Beyond their share, anyone can give extra to one of two places, and always chooses which:

- **The platform**: goes into the reserve and lowers every seller's share.
- **The developers**: goes to the people building IME (at launch, its creator) to support their work on it.

How people can give:

- A seller can add an optional extra amount to any top-up or to their auto top-up, and choose where it goes. The extra amount always defaults to zero.
- Anyone, with or without an account, can give on the donation page, once or monthly, and split one gift between the two destinations however they like.
- A giver can ask to be thanked by a name of their choosing on the public thank-you list for that month. The list shows names only, alphabetically, never amounts or destinations. Everyone who gives gets a thank-you email.

### The reserve

- The platform keeps a reserve of three months of its costs, which covers months when costs spike, so sellers' shares stay steady.
- Platform donations, rounding surplus, and money IME puts in itself (recorded by an admin, as at launch) go into it.
- At each monthly close, whatever it holds past three months of costs pays sellers' shares: every share in full when it can, otherwise the same part of each. Statements show what the reserve paid. A seller whose whole share was paid skips that month's monthly auto top-up.
- While it holds enough past its target to pay a whole month of shares, nobody needs a card or a top-up to start selling.
- When a close finds it can no longer pay the coming month, every seller whose balance would not cover their share is emailed a month ahead to add a card and top up. From then on the usual alerts and the grace period apply.

## Web app

### For sellers

- Balance, top-ups, auto top-up, alert threshold, card on file, and statements.
- For each artist they manage, how its costs are split, and accepting or changing a share.
- These are management concerns, available to every manager of an artist and to each designer for their own designs.

### Public costs page

- Leads with what every seller paid toward shared costs last month, the same for all, and the typical seller's whole share.
- How long the smallest top-up lasts the typical seller, and that there is no cut of sales.
- The month in full: what it cost, by category (storage and delivery, hosting, database, other services, ownership guarantee), and who paid it (sellers, and the reserve when it paid shares).
- The reserve against its target, gifts to hosting and money IME added that month, and what it paid toward sellers' shares. In months it paid them, the page leads with that instead.
- This month's thank-you list.
- A rough estimate of what a seller would pay, from how many songs, releases, and plays they have, at the latest rates.
- Earlier months in a table.
- Why there is no catch: no cut, no ads, no paid placement, every bill shown, and the code is public.
- A plain statement that none of the platform's money goes to the platform, its developers, or its founder as profit. Developer support is personal and is not shown.
- Ways in: selling on IME, and chipping in.

### Donation page

- Anyone can give once or monthly, choosing one amount and how it splits between the platform and the developers. Givers are only named if they ask to be.
- The developer option is presented quietly and plainly as optional, never pre-selected: a gift starts all to hosting.

## Mobile app

- Balances, top-ups, and statements are seller concerns and are **not** available on mobile.
- Listeners are unaffected. The public costs page and donation page are linked from the app and open on the web.

## Rules & constraints

- Over time, what sellers contribute never exceeds what the platform actually costs.
- Contributions are always billed separately from sales. The payment for a sale goes entirely and directly to the artist or designer.
- A seller's payment status never affects what listeners already own.
- Platform money and developer support are kept apart. Neither is ever moved into the other.
- Shares are always computed from real costs, and statements show the inputs so the math can be checked.

## How we talk about it

Public copy describes IME as **built for, funded by, and hosted by the community**, at cost. It explains that sellers chip in for what they use and that nobody behind IME profits from those contributions, while artists and designers keep what they earn. It says "IME's founder" or "IME's developers", never "creators", which on a music platform means the artists.

IME's voice can be bold about what it stands for and against: the culture of music and the people who make it, who should be paid what their work is worth, and against a platform cut, shareholders, and AI-generated music ("A music platform for the culture."). It makes that case with plain facts, such as a receipt showing where a sale's money goes, rather than slogans or party-political labels, and without guilt or a fundraising tone. Comparisons with other platforms name them and use their own published figures (for example, Bandcamp's published average share for artists).

Supporting the developers is mentioned subtly, in a few quiet places such as the donation page and the top-up form, and always as completely optional. It is never pushed in a seller's or listener's way.

IME launches as an LLC run at cost, with the aim of becoming a nonprofit as it grows. Until it is one, public copy never calls IME a nonprofit or describes donations as tax-deductible.

## Out of scope / future

- Per-feature pricing tiers or paid upgrades. There is one model: at cost.
- Advertising or selling listener data as a funding source.
- Named support tiers.
- Giving to charities through IME.
- Becoming a nonprofit.
- Reading the month's bills straight from the providers instead of entering them at the monthly close: Cloudflare R2's usage for storage and delivery, DigitalOcean's billing API for hosting, and fixed amounts for the database, email, and domain. To build once IME is deployed on DigitalOcean with its production bucket.
